# Character Cartoon Style

## Purpose

Create humorous, expressive, and visually consistent cartoon illustrations suitable for educational and entertainment videos.

## Visual Style

- 2D digital cartoon illustration.
- Thick, smooth black outlines.
- Clean and simplified geometric shapes.
- Large heads and compact bodies.
- Expressive facial features.
- Bright, saturated colors.
- Flat coloring with minimal cel shading.
- Playful and exaggerated body language.
- Clean silhouettes and readable compositions.
- Minimal unnecessary visual details.

## Character Rendering

- Preserve the identity of the supplied character.
- Use character templates and reference images when available.
- Keep facial structure, hairstyle, and body proportions consistent.
- Exaggerate expressions without changing character identity.
- Use natural cartoon anatomy.
- Avoid duplicated limbs, ears, fingers, or facial features.
- Keep accessories and clothing visually readable.

## Expressions

Support a wide range of emotions:

- Happiness
- Sadness
- Anger
- Fear
- Surprise
- Confusion
- Disgust
- Embarrassment
- Suspicion
- Excitement
- Exhaustion
- Sarcasm
- Confidence
- Panic

Use facial expressions, body gestures, and comic symbols to communicate emotions clearly.

## Composition

- Make the character the primary visual focus.
- Use clear and readable poses.
- Use simple backgrounds unless the scene requires detail.
- Avoid unnecessary objects and visual clutter.
- Keep important elements inside the frame.
- Ensure the illustration remains readable at thumbnail size.

## Scene Adaptation

Adapt the character to the scene using:

- Context-specific costumes.
- Relevant props.
- Appropriate facial expressions.
- Dynamic poses.
- Historical or fictional settings.
- Supporting cartoon characters.
- Educational visual elements.

## Character Consistency

When a character template is provided, follow its identity rules.

The style controls visual rendering, not character identity.

Never replace the supplied character with another mascot or a generic character.

## Avoid

- Photorealistic rendering.
- Realistic human anatomy.
- Complex textures.
- Heavy gradients.
- Excessive lighting effects.
- Unnecessary 3D rendering.
- Inconsistent outline thickness.
- Unrequested character redesigns.
- Watermarks and unwanted text.

## Prompt Suffix

Expressive 2D cartoon illustration, thick clean black outlines,
bright flat colors, minimal cel shading, exaggerated facial
expressions, playful body language, simplified geometric shapes,
clean silhouette, humorous educational cartoon aesthetic,
consistent character identity, professional digital illustration.

## HTML animation adaptation

For Xeko previews, use transparent static sprites for the character and
animate supporting objects independently. Use the requested canvas size;
the example preview is 1920×1080 even when a source storyboard is vertical.
Keep narration/player controls outside the animation canvas. Match timeline
beats to measured voice segments; preserve backward seeking and replay.
Use 5–8 px outlines for supporting SVG objects on a 1080 px canvas and
96 px margins for important content. Intentional character close-ups and
full-bleed scenery may extend beyond those margins.
